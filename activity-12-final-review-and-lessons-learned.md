## 3. Key Knowledge Gained

### Phishing Recognition

I learned to identify common phishing indicators, including urgent deadlines, threats, requests for credentials, suspicious links, unexpected attachments and attempts to discourage independent verification.

### Sender Analysis

I learned that a display name alone does not prove a sender's identity. Suspicious sender details should be assessed carefully, and unexpected requests should be verified through a trusted channel.

### Link and Attachment Safety

I learned not to open unverified links or unexpected attachments. A familiar-looking filename or HTTPS connection does not, by itself, guarantee safety.

### Social Engineering

I learned how attackers may use fear, urgency, authority, secrecy and impersonation to pressure people into making unsafe decisions.

### Email Authentication

I studied three important email authentication technologies:

* **SPF:** Checks whether a sending server is authorised under a domain's SPF policy.
* **DKIM:** Uses digital signatures to support email authentication and message integrity.
* **DMARC:** Checks alignment with the visible sender domain and provides policies and reporting for handling authentication failures.

I also learned that email authentication does not guarantee that a message is harmless.

### Incident Reporting

I practised organising findings, assessing potential risk, explaining investigation limitations and recommending appropriate security actions.

## 6. Overall Project Findings

The exercises demonstrated that phishing assessment requires considering multiple indicators together rather than relying on a single clue.

A suspicious email should be assessed using the available evidence, including its sender details, subject, content, links, attachment context and requested actions. Where legitimacy cannot be established, the recipient should avoid interacting with the message and verify the request independently.

Clear documentation helps communicate risks and guide appropriate defensive action.

## 7. Safety and Scope

All scenarios in this project were fictional. The exercises focused on defensive learning, conceptual analysis and written documentation.

No live malicious links were opened, no real suspicious attachments were executed, and no unauthorised accounts or systems were accessed.

This project was a learning exercise. It did not involve building or deploying an automated phishing detection system.
