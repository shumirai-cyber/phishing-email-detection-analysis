# Activity 10: Analysing a Simulated Phishing Email

## 1. Objective

To conduct a structured analysis of a fictional suspicious email, identify phishing indicators, assess potential risks and recommend appropriate security actions.

## 2. Email Summary

**Sender display name:** Account Security Team
**Sender address:** [account-security@example.test](mailto:account-security@example.test)
**Recipient:** Customer
**Subject:** URGENT: Your account will be permanently suspended today!

The email claims that unusual activity has been detected and threatens permanent account suspension unless the recipient confirms their identity within 30 minutes. It directs the recipient to an unverified link and requests their username and password. It also tells the recipient not to contact customer support.

## 3. Evidence and Warning Signs

### Finding 1: Urgent Subject Line

The subject uses urgent wording and threatens permanent suspension.

**Risk:** The recipient may act quickly without checking whether the message is genuine.

### Finding 2: Unverified Sender Identity

The sender claims to represent an account security team, but the display name and address alone do not establish legitimacy.

**Risk:** The recipient may trust an impersonated identity.

### Finding 3: Request for Login Credentials

The message directs the recipient to enter a username and password through an unverified link.

**Risk:** Credentials could be stolen and used to attempt unauthorised account access.

### Finding 4: Suspicious Link

The email directs the recipient to:

`https://account.example.test/verify`

The link was not opened or tested. Its presence in the message is a reason to investigate safely, not proof by itself that the destination is malicious.

**Risk:** Following an unverified link could expose the recipient to credential theft or another malicious activity.

### Finding 5: Secrecy and Discouraging Verification

The recipient is instructed not to contact customer support.

**Risk:** This may discourage independent verification and isolate the recipient from legitimate assistance.

## 4. Social Engineering Techniques

The email uses several common social engineering techniques:

* **Fear:** Threatening permanent account suspension.
* **Urgency:** Imposing a 30-minute deadline.
* **Impersonation:** Claiming to represent an account security team.
* **Credential harvesting:** Requesting a username and password.
* **Secrecy:** Discouraging contact with customer support.

Together, these techniques attempt to pressure the recipient into acting before verifying the message.

## 5. Risk Assessment

**Overall assessment: High potential risk**

The email contains multiple strong phishing indicators, particularly the urgent threat, request for credentials, unverified link and instruction to avoid customer support.

This assessment is based on the fictional message's content. The sender, destination and actual malicious activity were not independently verified.

## 6. Recommended Actions

1. Do not click the link or enter credentials.
2. Access the relevant service using its known official website or app.
3. Verify the alleged security alert through an official support channel.
4. Report the email using the organisation's approved phishing-reporting process.
5. If credentials have already been entered, use a trusted device and official channel to change the affected password, review account activity and follow the service's recovery guidance.
6. If the same password is used elsewhere, change it on those accounts too.
7. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.

## 7. Key Findings

The email combines urgency, fear, impersonation, credential requests and secrecy to encourage unsafe behaviour. No single indicator proves phishing in every situation, but the combination of indicators makes this message highly suspicious.

All five questions were answered correctly.

## 9. Scope and Safety

This exercise used a fictional email and the reserved example.test domain. The link was not opened, no messages were sent, and no real accounts or systems were accessed.

## 10. Conclusion

The simulated email demonstrates how phishing messages manipulate recipients into disclosing sensitive information. A structured review of the sender, subject, content, links and requested actions helps identify warning signs. Independent verification and timely reporting reduce the risk of account compromise.
