# Activity 11: Writing Findings and Security Recommendations

## 1. Objective

To practise documenting a suspected phishing incident in a professional format, distinguish evidence from assumptions, assess potential risks and provide clear, actionable security recommendations.

## 2. Incident Summary

**Incident type:** Suspected phishing email
**Severity assessment:** High potential risk; further investigation required
**Environment:** Fictional training scenario
**Confirmed compromise:** None identified in the information provided

An email claiming to come from an account security team threatened account suspension within 30 minutes. It requested the recipient's username and password through an unverified link and instructed the recipient not to contact customer support.

## 3. Findings

### Finding 1: Urgent Threat

The message threatened account suspension within a short deadline.

**Evidence:** The email stated that action was required within 30 minutes.

**Risk:** Urgency may pressure recipients into acting without verifying the message.

### Finding 2: Request for Credentials

The email requested a username and password.

**Evidence:** The recipient was instructed to enter login credentials through an unverified link.

**Risk:** If the recipient complied, their credentials could be exposed to an unauthorised party.

### Finding 3: Unverified Sender Identity

The sender claimed to represent an account security team, but the identity was not confirmed.

**Evidence:** The message's claimed identity had not been independently verified.

**Risk:** The sender could be impersonating a legitimate organisation.

### Finding 4: Unverified Link

The email included a link to an account verification page.

**Evidence:** The message directed the recipient to a destination that had not been independently verified.

**Risk:** Following the link could expose the recipient to credential theft or other malicious activity.

### Finding 5: Discouraging Independent Verification

The message instructed the recipient not to contact customer support.

**Evidence:** The email asked the recipient to keep the process confidential.

**Risk:** This could prevent the recipient from checking the request with a legitimate support team.

## 4. Overall Risk Assessment

**Assessment: High potential risk**

The combination of urgency, a credential request, an unverified link, an unconfirmed sender and secrecy instructions strongly suggests a phishing attempt.

However, the available information does not prove who sent the email or whether a real account was compromised.

## 5. Security Recommendations

### Immediate Actions

1. Advise recipients not to click the link or enter credentials.
2. Report the suspicious message using the organisation's approved reporting process.
3. Verify the claimed security alert through a known official website, app or support channel.
4. Ask the security team to assess the message and determine whether further investigation is needed.

### If Credentials Were Entered

1. Change the affected password through the legitimate service using a trusted device.
2. Review account activity and sign-in alerts.
3. Enable multi-factor authentication if available.
4. Change reused passwords on other accounts.
5. Follow the service's official account recovery guidance and notify the security team.

### Longer-Term Improvements

* Provide staff training on phishing and social engineering.
* Maintain a simple process for reporting suspicious emails.
* Use appropriate email filtering and authentication controls.
* Encourage staff to verify unexpected requests independently.
* Review reported incidents for recurring patterns and opportunities to improve security.

## 6. Evidence and Limitations

The assessment is based only on the fictional email details provided in this exercise.

No live link was opened, no email headers were examined, and no real accounts or systems were accessed. There is no evidence in this scenario that the recipient clicked the link or entered credentials.

Further technical investigation would be needed to establish the sender's identity, assess the destination and determine whether any compromise occurred.
