# Activity 1: Understanding Phishing Attacks

## 1. Objective
To understand what phishing is, why cybercriminals use it, and how to recognise common warning signs in suspicious emails.

## 2. What Is Phishing?
Phishing is a type of cyberattack in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organisation to trick someone into revealing sensitive information, clicking a malicious link, opening a harmful attachment, or sending money.

## 3. Scenario Analysed
I reviewed a fictional training email claiming that a Microsoft account would be deleted unless the recipient verified their password within two hours. The message used a generic greeting and urged the recipient to click a verification link.

This was a simulated example. No real email was sent, and no link was opened.

## 4. Warning Signs Identified
1. Urgent language: the message threatened account deletion within two hours.
2. Generic greeting: it addressed the recipient as “Dear User”.
3. Password request: it pressured the recipient to verify their password.
4. Verification link: it encouraged the recipient to click a link without independently verifying the request.
5. Unverified sender: the displayed sender name alone did not prove the message came from Microsoft.

## 5. Recommended Security Actions
- Do not click links or open attachments in unexpected messages.
- Never provide a password in response to an unsolicited email request.
- Check the sender's full email address and examine the message for inconsistencies.
- Visit the organisation's official website using a known address or bookmark instead of the email link.
- Report suspected phishing using the organisation's approved reporting process.

## 6. Key Learning
I learned that phishing often relies on urgency, impersonation, and pressure to make people act before checking whether a message is genuine. A single warning sign does not prove an email is malicious, so messages should be assessed carefully using several indicators.

## 7. Activity Result
The simulated email contained five warning signs that warranted further investigation. This was a desktop review of a fictional scenario, not a live email investigation.

## 8. Skills Developed
- Phishing awareness
- Identifying social engineering indicators
- Security risk assessment
- Defensive security recommendations
- Cybersecurity documentation
