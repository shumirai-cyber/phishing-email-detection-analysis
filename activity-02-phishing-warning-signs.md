# Activity 2: Identifying Common Phishing Warning Signs

## 1. Objective
To learn how to recognise common indicators of phishing in email messages and understand why suspicious messages require careful investigation.

## 2. Background
Phishing messages often use impersonation, urgency, unexpected payment requests, suspicious links, or requests for sensitive information. Individual warning signs do not always prove a message is fraudulent, so context and verification are important.

## 3. Scenario Analysed
I reviewed a fictional email claiming that a parcel could not be delivered until a £1.99 redelivery fee was paid. The message used a generic greeting, imposed a 30-minute deadline, and directed the recipient to a payment link.

This was a simulated example. No real sender was contacted and no payment link was opened.

## 4. Warning Signs Identified
1. Urgency and pressure: the 30-minute deadline encouraged a rushed decision.
2. Unexpected payment request: the recipient was asked to pay a redelivery fee they might not have been expecting.
3. Generic greeting: the message used “Hello Customer” rather than identifying the recipient.
4. Payment link: the message encouraged the recipient to follow a link instead of independently checking the delivery status.
5. Unverified sender: the display name “Delivery Support” did not establish the sender's identity.

## 5. Recommended Security Actions
- Do not click links in unexpected delivery messages.
- Check tracking information through the delivery company's official website or app using a known address.
- Verify whether a fee is genuinely due before making any payment.
- Check the sender's full email address, while remembering that addresses can also be spoofed or compromised.
- Report suspected phishing using the appropriate email provider or organisation reporting process.

## 6. Key Learning
I learned that phishing messages can imitate routine services such as parcel delivery. Urgency, unexpected fees, generic greetings, payment links, and unverified sender identities are useful warning signs. A small payment amount does not make a request trustworthy.

## 7. Activity Result
Five potential warning signs were identified in the fictional delivery email. The scenario was reviewed using the message text only; no live email or website was investigated.

## 8. Skills Developed
- Recognising phishing indicators
- Assessing suspicious payment requests
- Understanding social engineering
- Recommending safe verification steps
- Documenting security findings

## 9. Status
Completed: introductory review of a simulated phishing email.

## 10. Safety Statement
This activity was completed for defensive cybersecurity education. The example was fictional, and no real links were clicked or payments made.
