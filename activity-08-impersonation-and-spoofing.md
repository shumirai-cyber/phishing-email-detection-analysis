# Activity 8: Recognising Impersonation and Spoofing

## Objective

To understand how cybercriminals impersonate trusted people and organisations to manipulate recipients into revealing sensitive information or carrying out fraudulent actions.

## Scenario 1: Fake IT Support Email

A fictional email claimed that an account would be disabled within 10 minutes unless the recipient immediately provided their password. It also instructed the recipient not to contact their manager.

### Warning Signs Identified

* **Urgency:** The 10-minute deadline pressured the recipient to act quickly.
* **Credential theft:** The sender requested a password.
* **Secrecy:** The recipient was told not to contact their manager.
* **Unverified identity:** The sender's identity had not been independently confirmed.

### Recommended Response

Do not reply with a password or use links supplied in the suspicious message. Verify the request through a known official IT support channel and report it according to workplace procedures.

## Scenario 2: Manager Impersonation

A fictional message appeared to come from a manager and urgently requested five gift cards and their codes. The recipient was instructed to keep the request confidential.

### Warning Signs Identified

* An urgent request involving money or gift cards.
* A request to disclose gift-card codes.
* Pressure to keep the transaction secret.
* An identity that had not been independently verified.

### Recommended Response

Do not purchase gift cards or send codes until the request has been verified. Contact the manager through a separate, trusted communication channel.

## Key Findings

1. Attackers may impersonate IT support, managers, banks or other trusted organisations.
2. Email display names can be misleading and do not prove a sender's identity.
3. Urgency, secrecy and requests for passwords or gift-card codes are important warning signs.
4. A message should be verified through a trusted channel independent of the suspicious message.
5. Suspicious messages should be reported using the appropriate security reporting process.

## Security Recommendations

* Never share passwords in response to an email or message.
* Verify unusual requests for money, sensitive information or urgent action.
* Check the full sender address, while remembering that an address alone may not prove legitimacy.
* Avoid opening unverified links or attachments.
* Report suspected impersonation attempts to the appropriate IT or security team.
* Follow organisational security policies and approved reporting procedures.

## Skills Demonstrated

* Recognising impersonation and spoofing attempts.
* Identifying social engineering warning signs.
* Evaluating suspicious requests.
* Applying independent verification methods.
* Recommending safe incident-reporting practices.
