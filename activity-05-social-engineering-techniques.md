# Activity 5: Recognising Social Engineering Techniques

## 1. Objective

The objective of this activity was to understand social engineering and identify techniques attackers use to manipulate people into revealing confidential information or taking unsafe actions.

I focused on impersonation, urgency, fear, requests for passwords and instructions to keep requests secret.

## 2. Simulated Scenario

I analysed a fictional message from a sender claiming to represent the IT Helpdesk.

The message stated that the recipient's work account would be disabled within 15 minutes unless they provided their password. It also instructed the recipient to keep the request confidential.

This was a simulated exercise. No real organisation or account was involved.

## 3. Social Engineering Techniques Identified

### Technique 1: Impersonation and False Authority

The sender claimed to be the IT Helpdesk.

**Analysis:** Attackers may pretend to be IT staff, managers, banks or other trusted people to make their requests seem legitimate.

**Risk:** The recipient might trust the sender without checking their identity.

### Technique 2: Urgency and Time Pressure

The sender gave the recipient only 15 minutes to respond.

**Analysis:** Short deadlines can pressure people into acting quickly instead of thinking carefully or verifying a request.

**Risk:** The recipient might disclose sensitive information before recognising the warning signs.

### Technique 3: Request for Sensitive Information

The sender asked the recipient to provide their password.

**Analysis:** This was a major security warning sign because the sender could be attempting to obtain login credentials.

**Risk:** A stolen password could allow unauthorised access to an account and potentially other connected systems.

### Technique 4: Secrecy and Isolation

The sender instructed the recipient to keep the request confidential.

**Analysis:** Attackers may discourage people from consulting colleagues, managers or security teams because independent verification could expose the deception.

**Risk:** The recipient might follow unsafe instructions without seeking advice.

### Technique 5: Fear of Losing Access

The message threatened to disable the recipient's account.

**Analysis:** The threat was designed to create anxiety and make the recipient believe immediate action was necessary.

**Risk:** Fear can reduce careful decision-making and make a person more vulnerable to manipulation.

## 4. Overall Risk Assessment

The simulated message contained several strong indicators of social engineering:

* A claim of authority that had not been verified
* A short deadline
* A request for a password
* An instruction to keep the request secret
* A threat of account deactivation

**Assessment:** The message was highly suspicious and should not be trusted without independent verification.

The assessment was based on the combination of warning signs, rather than the sender's claimed identity alone.

## 5. Recommended Security Actions

If I received a similar message, I would:

1. Never share my password with the sender.
2. Verify the request using a known, trusted IT support channel.
3. Avoid replying to the suspicious message or following any links it contains.
4. Report the message through the organisation's approved security-reporting procedure.
5. Contact a manager or security team if the request appears to impersonate a member of staff.
6. Follow organisational security policies when handling suspected social engineering attempts.

## 6. Key Lessons Learned

This activity taught me that social engineering attacks target human behaviour as well as technical systems.

I learned that attackers may use authority, urgency, fear and secrecy together to influence their victims. Requests for passwords and attempts to prevent independent verification should be treated with particular caution.

I also learned the importance of verifying identities through trusted channels before following unusual instructions.

## 7. Skills Demonstrated

* Social engineering awareness
* Recognition of impersonation techniques
* Identification of urgency and fear-based manipulation
* Protection of sensitive information
* Basic security risk assessment
* Documentation of security findings and recommendations

## 8. Safety and Scope

This activity used a fictional message for educational purposes. No real passwords or confidential information were shared, and no real organisation or account was contacted. The exercise was conducted safely as part of a beginner cybersecurity portfolio.

## 9. Conclusion

Recognising social engineering techniques is an important cybersecurity skill because attackers often exploit trust and human emotions to bypass security controls.

By identifying impersonation, urgency, secrecy and requests for sensitive information, I can make safer decisions and recommend appropriate actions to reduce security risks.
