# Activity 4: Analysing Email Subject Lines and Message Content

## 1. Objective

The objective of this activity was to learn how to analyse email subject lines and message content to identify possible phishing indicators. I focused on urgent language, threats, generic greetings, requests for passwords and suspicious instructions.

## 2. Simulated Email Analysis

I examined a fictional email claiming that the recipient's account would be permanently deleted unless they verified their details within 30 minutes.

### Subject Line

**"URGENT!!! Your account will be permanently deleted TODAY!"**

### Observations

* The word "URGENT" was used to create a sense of emergency.
* Capital letters and multiple exclamation marks made the subject line more alarming.
* The threat of permanent account deletion could cause fear.
* The subject line encouraged the recipient to act quickly instead of checking whether the email was genuine.

**Finding:** The subject line contained urgency and fear-based language, which are common warning signs in phishing attempts.

## 3. Analysis of the Message Body

### Warning Sign 1: Generic Greeting

The message began with "Dear Customer".

This greeting did not identify the recipient. Although legitimate organisations may use generic greetings, it can be a warning sign when combined with other suspicious features.

### Warning Sign 2: Threat of Account Deletion

The message claimed that the account would be permanently deleted within 30 minutes.

This threat was designed to create panic and discourage the recipient from taking time to verify the claim.

### Warning Sign 3: Immediate Action Required

The recipient was told to verify their details immediately.

Phishing emails often use short deadlines to pressure people into acting without considering the risks.

### Warning Sign 4: Request for a Password

The email instructed the recipient to enter their password through a verification link.

This was a significant security concern because a fraudulent page could collect the recipient's login credentials.

### Warning Sign 5: Unverified Claim

The message claimed that there was a serious problem with the account, but the email itself did not establish whether the claim was genuine.

The account status should be checked independently through the organisation's official website or app.

## 4. Risk Assessment

The simulated email contained several warning signs:

* Urgent and alarming subject line
* Threat of permanent account deletion
* Short deadline designed to create pressure
* Generic greeting
* Request to enter a password through an email link
* Unverified account-security claim

**Overall assessment:** The email was highly suspicious and should not be trusted without independent verification.

This assessment was based on the combination of indicators rather than any single feature.

## 5. Recommended Security Actions

If I received a similar email, I would:

1. Avoid clicking links or opening unexpected attachments.
2. Never enter my password through an unverified email link.
3. Open the organisation's official website or app independently to check the account.
4. Avoid replying with sensitive personal information.
5. Report the email using the organisation's approved phishing-reporting process.
6. Follow workplace or organisational security procedures where applicable.

## 6. Key Lessons Learned

This activity helped me understand that phishing emails can manipulate people through fear, urgency and threats.

I learned to examine both the subject line and the message body, paying attention to the language used, the actions requested and whether the claims can be independently verified.

I also learned that individual warning signs do not automatically prove an email is malicious. Multiple indicators should be considered together before reaching an assessment.

## 7. Skills Demonstrated

* Email content analysis
* Identification of phishing indicators
* Recognition of social engineering techniques
* Basic risk assessment
* Security awareness and safe verification
* Documentation of findings and recommendations

## 8. Safety and Scope

This was a simulated educational exercise. The email was fictional, and no real verification links were opened, no credentials were entered, and no attachments were downloaded. The activity was conducted for cybersecurity learning purposes only.
