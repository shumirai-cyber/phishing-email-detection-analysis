# Activity 6: Safely Inspecting Suspicious Links

## 1. Objective

The objective of this activity was to learn how to examine URLs safely and identify characteristics that may indicate a phishing attempt.

I focused on identifying actual domains, recognising misleading addresses, understanding shortened links and learning why HTTPS does not guarantee that a website is trustworthy.

## 2. Practical Exercise

I examined three fictional URL examples without opening or visiting them.

### Example A: Familiar-Looking URL

**URL:** `https://account.example.com/security`

**Observations:**

* The URL uses HTTPS.
* The hostname is `account.example.com`.
* The structure is readable and includes a security-related path.
* The appearance of the URL alone does not establish that a website is trustworthy.

**Finding:** A URL should not be trusted solely because it looks professional or uses HTTPS.

### Example B: Misleading Company Name

**URL:** `https://microsoft.example.test/login`

**Observations:**

* The hostname contains the word `microsoft`.
* The actual domain in this fictional example is `example.test`, not Microsoft's official domain.
* A familiar company name can appear as part of a hostname without the address belonging to that company.
* The recipient should not assume that the link is legitimate based on the company name.

**Finding:** Identifying the actual domain is essential when examining potentially misleading URLs.

### Example C: Shortened-Looking URL

**URL:** `https://short.example.test/a7x2`

**Observations:**

* The path is short and does not clearly describe the destination.
* The URL does not provide enough information to establish what content would appear if it were opened.
* Shortened or unfamiliar URLs can make it harder to recognise the destination before visiting it.
* The URL would require appropriate verification before it could be trusted.

**Finding:** Unexpected shortened links should be treated cautiously, especially when received in suspicious messages.

*Note: These were fictional examples used for educational purposes. No websites were opened or visited.*

## 3. Key Findings

The exercise demonstrated several important security principles:

1. **Examine the actual domain.** A familiar company name within a URL does not prove that the address belongs to that company.
2. **HTTPS is not proof of legitimacy.** It helps encrypt the connection between a browser and a website, but malicious websites can also use HTTPS.
3. **Shortened links can obscure destinations.** Their destination may not be obvious from the displayed URL.
4. **URL appearance can be misleading.** Professional-looking addresses and website designs do not guarantee safety.
5. **Use independent verification.** Unexpected account or payment requests should be checked through a known official website or app.
6. **Avoid unnecessary exposure.** Suspicious links should not be opened simply to see where they lead.

## 4. Security Recommendations

If I receive a suspicious link, I should:

* Avoid clicking unexpected links in emails or messages.
* Read the hostname carefully and identify the actual domain.
* Look for misspellings, misleading subdomains and unfamiliar domains.
* Avoid entering passwords, payment information or other sensitive data into unverified websites.
* Access services through a trusted bookmark or an official address obtained independently.
* Follow the organisation's approved procedure for reporting suspicious messages.
* Use approved security tools and procedures when further URL investigation is required.

## 5. Key Lessons Learned

This activity improved my understanding of how phishing attackers can use misleading URLs to make unsafe destinations appear trustworthy.

I learned to distinguish a company name from the actual domain, to treat shortened links cautiously and to understand that HTTPS does not establish whether a website is legitimate.

I also learned that safe investigation does not require clicking a suspicious link. Reviewing the visible URL and verifying unexpected requests independently are important first steps.

## 6. Skills Demonstrated

* Basic URL analysis
* Domain identification
* Recognition of misleading URLs
* Phishing risk awareness
* Safe security investigation
* Documentation of findings and recommendations

## 7. Safety and Scope

This was a simulated educational exercise using fictional URLs. No suspicious links were opened, no websites were visited and no personal information or credentials were entered. The activity was conducted for beginner cybersecurity learning purposes only.

## 8. Conclusion

Safely inspecting URLs is an important cybersecurity skill because phishing attacks often rely on misleading links to direct people towards fraudulent websites.

By examining domains carefully, understanding the limitations of HTTPS and verifying unexpected requests independently, I can reduce the risk of interacting with malicious links.
